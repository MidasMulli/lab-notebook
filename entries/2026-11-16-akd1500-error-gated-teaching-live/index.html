An AKD1500 uses prediction errors to gate teaching, filmed live

2026-11-16 · AKD1500on-chip learningprediction

The AKD1500 can use its own prediction errors to gate teaching. In this live repeat of a fixed binary-stream test, it opened 2 of 48 teaching gates and recovered both changed answers. Always-teach opened 48; cutting the gate or disabling learning kept the old answers. Holding history fixed caused errors.

Numbers

One synthetic stream of 48 next-bit predictions, with the rule changing two answers after step 16, run under five conditions:

Every output and every measured memory snapshot in all five conditions matched the earlier physical run byte for byte. The earlier study ran six preselected synthetic streams; error-gated teaching opened 2 of 48 gates and recovered both changed answers in all six.

The part worth knowing

In the error-gated condition, the teaching gate opens only when the prediction and supplied observation disagree. It recovers the same changed answers as always-teach, with 2 gate openings instead of 48. The short history and comparison run as programmed circuits on the chip; the host supplies the stream, initializes the rule and reads results back.

Limits

  • We supply the stream, the starting history and the initial rule. The history and error circuits are programmed on the chip, not learned.
  • This is a repeat of one previously tested synthetic stream, not a new task or a representative sample.
  • Gate openings are not connection writes: error-gated and always-teach each changed connections twice in the reference trace.
  • No timing advantage and no energy saving is claimed or measured.

Evidence and availability

  • The November release notes describe the original six-stream study (prediction-error gated teaching).
  • The run record, raw readbacks and independent recount are kept with the lab's internal audit; the video above is a transcode of the complete certified screen-recording master, with no cuts or added overlays.
